# AI contracts

AI providers support learner-facing transformations such as study-guide generation, tutoring, quiz creation, marking, and translation. They are not PDF text extractors, PDF extractor routers, or the default material relevance classifier. Use local tools for selectable PDF text and Mathpix for scanned content, STEM notation, and relevant diagrams. Material-to-goal relevance starts with a deterministic local text match. Uncertain pages remain reviewable by the learner without a paid model call.
Version prompts and schemas. Validate at provider boundary and before persistence.

## Concept extraction
Input: normalized chunks with immutable IDs.
Output: concepts with temporary IDs, names, descriptions, source chunk IDs; dependencies referencing temporary concept IDs.
Validate nonempty source refs, known chunk IDs, duplicate consolidation, no self-dependencies, and acyclic graph. Mark inferred prerequisites separately from source-supported relationships.

## Tutor
Input: question, up to six recent messages from the same learner-owned tutor thread, optional selected concept ID, source chunks linked to that concept when selected (otherwise bounded learner-space retrieval), learner evidence, desired language.
Output: a short guided lesson or context-aware response, citations with chunk ID and supported excerpt, supplementary sections, insufficient_evidence flag, and an `understanding_check` enum (`not_applicable`, `understood`, `developing`, `needs_support`). First lessons teach the idea in three short connected steps and end with one check question. Follow-ups explicitly assess the learner's reply to the prior check, explain corrections, and give one next question or action without restarting the lesson. Persist the qualitative outcome only for concept-targeted tutor turns and use the latest outcome to recommend another explanation when needed. This signal is formative, not a numeric score or mastery claim.
The current reply-language preference is scoped to a learning space and applies to new tutor replies only. The Nigeria-focused choices are English, Hausa, Yoruba, and Igbo. Tag each saved conversation turn with its selected language; when the learner changes languages, do not include previous turns in a different language as model conversation context. The current selection overrides previous reply language and question language. Offering a language is not a claim of equivalent quality; each language needs learner-reviewed quality evaluation. Keep source citation excerpts verbatim in their source language. Do not translate, rewrite, or normalize source evidence as part of a localized response. Missing-evidence replies and citation-verification recovery copy use deterministic text in the selected supported language, without another provider request. An unsupported saved language falls back to English. The localized recovery prompt does not translate source excerpts or reuse an unverified answer.
Reject nonexistent references. When a model quote matches punctuation, case, or OCR line-wrap variants, persist and display the exact source substring. If the model paraphrases its quote, display a bounded verbatim excerpt from the identified learner-owned chunk instead of its paraphrase. This keeps the visible citation exact without rejecting an otherwise useful lesson. Citation existence alone does not prove claim support: include a small grounding evaluation set.

For Mathpix image OCR, preserve `confidence` and `confidence_rate` as distinct fields. Do not interpret raw `confidence` as a page-quality percentage; Mathpix describes it as a product of token confidence values. If the page-level `confidence_rate` is missing or below the configured provisional threshold, keep the extracted page available for owner review but exclude it from relevance classification and concept extraction until the learner compares the transcription with the original.

Clock diagrams have a separate, versioned local visual reader because Mathpix text confidence does not establish that clock hands were interpreted. Persist its candidates and bounds separately from OCR. Hold any page with detected clocks out of relevance and concept generation until the material owner checks or corrects every time against the original. Only confirmed values may be appended to the chunk, with an explicit label that they are learner-confirmed visual readings, not Mathpix text. If AI concept generation returns no concept for confirmed clock evidence, a small deterministic fallback may create a source-linked clock concept; its general teaching guidance must be labeled supplementary and must use only owner-confirmed times. Do not generalize this clock-specific reader to other diagrams or STEM figures.

Strict JSON schema output validates response shape, not factual grounding. For confirmed clock readings, server validation downgrades generated hour-hand or minute-hand position claims to supplementary when the cited chunk contains no hand-position evidence. The same basis is applied when displaying older saved assessment questions, and exam mode rejects these supplementary questions. This is a narrow regression guard, not a general semantic fact checker.

## Quiz
Input: target concept, source chunks, requested count and level.
Output: MCQs and one written question with prompt, option IDs/text where applicable, one correct option ID for MCQs, a short explanation, two to seven ordered solution steps, knowledge basis (`source_supported` or `supplementary`), concept ID, and topic-source refs. Keep solution steps private until the learner submits. For quantitative questions, show the principle, formula, known values, substitution with units, calculation, and final answer. For conceptual questions, explain the reasoning sequence and why the correct option fits. Written questions also receive a model answer in ordered steps aligned to the marking rubric. Use concrete examples from the learner's material and explain them in familiar terms. Keep internal processing notes and implementation details out of learner-facing prompts, feedback, and worked answers. Show exact source evidence separately as a citation, distinct from the teaching explanation. Every analytic rubric criterion includes an exact `prompt_reference` phrase copied from an explicit requirement in the question. Specific dates, numbers, people, or labels cannot be required by a criterion unless the question explicitly asks for that detail. If a source-supported numeric criterion is otherwise hidden, make the request explicit in the question and point the reference to that added requirement. Retry one rejected question draft, then fall back to a source-based two-fact explanation task rather than blocking practice. The marker independently returns `needs_review` without a score if it detects any remaining hidden rubric requirement.
Use natural, self-contained exam-style questions that test understanding or application instead of asking learners to locate wording in an unnamed extract. Write mathematical expressions in LaTeX with `\(...\)` inline or `\[...\]` display delimiters so the app can render them accessibly. `source_supported` means the answer and explanation are directly supported by cited chunks. `supplementary` permits carefully bounded, standard subject knowledge when a source names a topic but lacks a readable explanation; the UI must clearly say the question goes beyond the learner's material. Its source refs identify only the material that establishes the topic, not evidence for the added facts. Never invent illegible source content or missing STEM values. Validate counts, unique options, exactly one valid key, basis, ownership and concept/source consistency. Never expose keys in the learner download pack.
Written questions follow the same basis rule and must never ask learners to evaluate scan/OCR quality, unreadable text, or the upload process. If a source identifies a topic without supporting a useful question, a supplemental exam-style problem may use established subject knowledge and must be labeled accordingly; otherwise do not generate an assessment question from uncertain content. Marking uses the question basis, and topic-source references for supplemental questions must not be presented as support for added facts.

## Past-paper question bank
Past-paper practice begins with page text from the configured local/Mathpix extraction router. Never send raw PDFs to a language model for text extraction. Parse extracted pages into source-preserving question records that retain exact OCR wording, paper/material identity, page and locator, question number/subpart, marks, options, and response format. Supported formats include single-answer MCQ, multi-select MCQ, true/false, fill-in-the-gap, and written/structured response. Preserve the original type; if OCR does not make the structure or answer choices clear, keep the question for learner review instead of fabricating a normalized version. Any diagram-dependent question must retain a resolvable source-page reference and must not be treated as diagram-understood when only OCR text is available.

The local question index uses deterministic boundary and format rules, not an AI extraction call. It indexes only related pages from materials marked as past exam papers, preserves question text and source page, stores detected response text separately from the prompt, and hides that response from ordinary material views. Examiner observations are not question text. Missing OCR confidence defaults to review-required. Candidate indexing is idempotent per source page and extraction version. The indexed list is a preview, not a complete or verified exam.

The playable flow lets the owner start a paper-level set from all current-extraction candidates marked ready in one uploaded paper. Candidates are ordered by page and question number; needs-review candidates are excluded. The run snapshots candidate IDs so a changed extraction cannot silently alter an in-progress paper. The exam-style UI shows one question at a time, offers question navigation and review flags, retains draft answers in session-scoped browser storage, and submits at the expiry of a learner-selected 30, 60, or 90 minute practice timer. This chosen timer is not official paper timing and is not proctored. Validate each response against its original format, permit unanswered questions, and save the run once with one owner-scoped result per answered question. Repeated submission returns the same result and cannot duplicate attempts. Do not expose detected responses before submission. Deterministic objective scoring is allowed only when the stored source response is an unambiguous option label that matches the stored options; label it as an unverified extracted response, not an official mark. Other formats and ambiguous keys are saved as ungraded. Never generate a replacement answer in this flow. After submission, show the response basis and source page; an uploaded marking scheme is still required for verified exam marking.

Mixed-paper practice may combine only ready past-paper uploads from the same learner-owned learning space. Snapshot the selected material IDs in learner-selected order and the current clear question IDs in paper order. Keep each question linked to its own material, upload-label year, page, and question number in practice and results. A year inferred from an upload title or filename is only a hint. Marks inferred from page text are not verified and must not be summed or used as score weights. Until official paper timing is explicitly matched to the selected papers, use only the learner-selected practice timer and label it as not official.

An uploaded marking scheme takes precedence for keys and worked solutions, with matching verified against the specific paper and question locator. If no key is supplied, generated answers and explanations are provisional and must remain distinct from source evidence. Do not claim a generated key is an official answer. Objective types with validated answer keys can be graded deterministically. Free-text and structured explanations use a private rubric whose criteria cover only requirements explicitly asked in that question. After submission, show the source paper/page, the learner response, the key/feedback basis, and a worked explanation. Never reveal answers before submission in timed practice.

## Failures
Timeout, malformed output, empty extraction, unsupported source and provider rate limit must leave originals intact. Use bounded retries; persist actionable status without raw sensitive provider payloads. Topic-launched tutor requests carry the selected concept ID so retrieval and any citation-failure fallback stay with that concept's source-linked chunks. For an untargeted question, only use a concept fallback when its full normalized name is explicitly present in the question and its sources are among the retrieved chunks. If the topic is ambiguous, do not guess or switch lessons; preserve the question and offer a retry or clear topic-selection action. On citation failure, English can use the stored source-linked concept explanation. Hausa, Yoruba, and Igbo use a deterministic, localized recovery prompt that points to exact passages for the selected concept and asks the learner to explain the idea; it makes no extra provider call and does not translate or rewrite citations. This recovery prompt is not presented as a translated concept explanation. Never present the rejected AI answer as verified. For missing evidence, save a localized explanation and a clear next action without calling the tutor provider. For other failures, preserve the question and give the learner a clear retry or next study action. For OpenAI failures, retain only sanitized HTTP status, provider error type/code, `Retry-After` seconds, and provider request ID. Never log the provider error message, prompt, request body, response body, or credentials. Do not retry known quota or spending-limit errors. Retry recognized transient rate limits only within a short bound, honor `Retry-After`, and use capped exponential backoff with jitter when the header is absent; surface longer waits and unknown 429 causes to the learner. Treat embedded instructions in uploaded documents as untrusted content.

## Evaluation
Fixtures: handwritten equations, printed science text, web-development notes, ambiguous OCR, adversarial document instructions and missing evidence. Verify grounded claims, domain independence, graph validity, correct grading and helpful refusal when sources are insufficient.

## Speech output
On explicit learner request, send only the visible text selected by that read-aloud control to the authenticated server speech endpoint. The server validates length and language, calls OpenAI text-to-speech, and returns audio. Cache the generated MP3 privately per learner for up to 30 days, keyed by a hash of the exact text, language, model, voice, and speech instructions. Never share cached audio between learners. Do not include unrelated page content or send answer keys before submission. Keep the full readable text available if generation or playback fails. Use a short, plain-language notice that the selected text is sent to create AI speech. Keep implementation details such as cache duration out of the read-aloud control. Document retention separately in the privacy notice. Speech quality and language availability require separate verification; do not imply parity across English, Hausa, Igbo, and Yoruba.

## Learning loop and progress
Use the latest submitted practice attempt per concept as a transparent planning signal. Combine server-graded MCQ points with AI-written points only when marking status is `marked`; pending, failed, and `needs_review` written responses are not zeroes. Keep path coverage (attempted concepts) separate from this score. Store tutor check outcomes separately as qualitative, formative signals. A `needs_support` tutor check recommends another explanation but does not lock navigation. A score below the current 70% review heuristic recommends another explanation or practice on that idea or a weak prerequisite. Only recommend prerequisites that have related, ready source material in this learner's path; otherwise point back to the current source-backed concept. Neither signal is mastery, and recommendations must be explained as suggestions. After showing worked feedback, link the learner directly to the recommended source lesson, tutor, and practice action.

## Extraction interoperability
Input chunks may originate from pdftotext, PHP parsing, local OCR or Mathpix. Preserve extractor/page metadata and LaTeX expressions; do not assume every input was processed by Mathpix. See docs/EXTRACTION_STACK.md.
